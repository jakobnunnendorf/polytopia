\section{Question and hypothesis}
\textbf{The idea (David, 27 Nov 2026, issue \#166).} Simplify both the state and the actions. State: (cities, income,
units, techs) instead of (turn, cities, income, units, levels, techs). Actions: only the ways to spend stars,
0 economy, 1 army, 2 research; expansion stays the bot's default. Background (experiments 21, 22): one turn's boost
moves the outcome by about 15 points against a spread of about 1{,}500, and with 3--4 visits per state and action the
$Q$ values differ mostly by chance. On the 300{,}000 random-action games of experiment 21, the four features give
57{,}029 states (144 visits per state, about 48 per action with three actions) instead of 506{,}245 (16 per state).
Dropping the turn merges situations several turns apart (4.5 turns between a state's first and last turn, on
average), so David's choice is to run both versions, with and without the turn, on the same data.

\textbf{Prediction} (before any run of this experiment):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Few, well-visited states.} Without the turn about 60{,}000 states, with it about 280{,}000; at min-count
        5 the policy has an entry for more than 97\% of visits without the turn, more than 90\% with it.
  \item \emph{The policy researches more.} Both chosen policies play research on more than 50\% of learner turns
        (21: 29.5\%; research is the only one of the three actions that helps when played every turn).
  \item \emph{The smaller state is the better one.} Without the turn the policy scores higher on the validation
        seeds than with it (more visits outweigh the lost information).
  \item \emph{Final test.} The better version passes the \#171 rule against \texttt{greedy-full-probe-perf} on the
        evaluation seeds with a mean gain of 200--450 points (21: +104), is better than 21's policy seed by seed, and
        comes within 150 points of research every turn (21: 352 behind).
  \item \emph{Known risk without the turn.} The model then has loops (a state can lead back to itself with a
        positive reward), so the values are only bounded by the cap of 30 sweeps where a loop is certain; the
        sweeps settle (last change below 1 point) at min-count 5 or more.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Against the idea:} if neither version beats 21's policy on the evaluation seeds, simplifying the state and
the actions does not help value iteration here.

\section{Mechanism}
\textbf{Actions} (\texttt{spend3} in \texttt{rl\_value::strategic}): 0 economy (build), 1 army (train, attack),
2 research; each multiplies the base bot's move-type factors of its types by 2 for one learner turn (the same
boosts as these three actions in experiments 15--22). There is no ``bot unchanged'' action: a state without a policy
entry plays the base bot unchanged (David's choice). Army keeps train and attack (David's choice).

\textbf{State} (\texttt{--features}): (cities, income, units, techs), and (turn, cities, income, units, techs).
Dropped features are 0 in the policy table and in the lookup.

\textbf{Value iteration} as in experiment 21 ($Q(s,a) = $ mean of $r + V(s')$, $V(s) = \max_{a: n(s,a) \ge m}
Q(s,a)$, fallback to the mean over all transitions, $\gamma = 1$). With the turn, one backward sweep from the last
turn is exact. Without it, the same projected state occurs at different turns and can lead back to itself, so the
sweeps repeat (Jacobi, from $V = 0$) until the largest change is below $10^{-6}$, at most 30 times (the game's length
in learner turns); the tool reports the number of sweeps and the last change.

\section{Setup}
As experiment 21 (decided gate setup on \texttt{dev} at da04376; learner base bot \texttt{greedy-full-probe-perf};
opponents probe-perf, combo-perf and the anchor probe-perf, shuffled per seed). Seeds: training $1\dots300{,}000$
(new games: the actions differ from 21's); action check $600{,}000{,}000\dots$ (2{,}000; the same seeds and boosts
as 21's check, so its numbers must repeat); validation $700{,}000{,}000\dots$ (2{,}000); evaluation $10^9\dots$
(10{,}000). The min-count is chosen per state version by the share of better validation seeds; both chosen policies
play the evaluation seeds, with \texttt{rl-gate} against the bot and with \texttt{rl-value eval} against research
every turn (\texttt{fixed:2}).

Sanity checks before the run: unit tests (spend3 weights; feature projection; an empty spend3 table ties its base bot
in \texttt{rl-gate}; value iteration without the turn reaches the fixed point of a self-loop; turns played by the
default count only in the fallback); \texttt{rl-value}'s bot games and 21's policy are unchanged by the new code
(500 seeds row for row; the policy file identical).
