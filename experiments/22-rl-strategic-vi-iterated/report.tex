\section{Question and hypothesis}
\textbf{The idea (David, 26 Nov 2026, issue \#166).} ``Maybe it's better to just run the experiment longer?'' In
experiment 21, more random-action data helped less and less: the validation gain of the policy against
\texttt{greedy-full-probe-perf} was +67, +137 and +154 points at 30{,}000, 100{,}000 and 300{,}000 games. The
explanation tested here: a research-heavy player reaches states (many techs for the turn) that uniformly random
play almost never visits (five research turns in a row happen in 1 of 3{,}125 stretches), so value iteration has no
data where a better policy goes. ``Running longer'' then means rounds of play and refit: play with the current
policy plus some random actions, refit the model on all data, run value iteration again, repeat. This is model-based
RL with iterated $\varepsilon$-greedy data collection (a Dyna-style loop of acting, learning the model and planning);
it resembles policy iteration in spirit, but the inner step is full value iteration on the learned model, not an
evaluation of the current policy.

\textbf{Prediction} (before the run):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{The rounds help.} The validation gain against the bot rises from round to round and reaches at least
        +250 points in round 4 (21: +154 on the same 2{,}000 seeds).
  \item \emph{More research.} The share of learner turns on which the policy plays research rises above 50\% by
        round 4 (21: 29.5\%).
  \item \emph{Less optimism.} $V$(start) minus the policy's real mean remaining score falls round by round, below
        +800 in round 4 (21: +1{,}793 at min-count 5).
  \item \emph{Final test.} On the 10{,}000 evaluation seeds the round-4 policy passes the \#171 rule against
        \texttt{greedy-full-probe-perf}, with a mean gain of 200--400 points; against research every turn it still
        does not pass, but the gap shrinks below 150 points (21: 352).
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Against the explanation:} if the validation gain does not rise over the rounds, missing data in
research-heavy states is not what holds value iteration back.

\section{Mechanism}
State, actions, boost and value iteration as in experiment 21 (\texttt{engine/rlvalue}: six exact integers; five
strategic actions that multiply the base bot's move-type factors by 2 for one turn; finite-horizon value iteration
over the learned model, min-count 5; a state without a policy entry plays the bot).

\textbf{Rounds.} Round 0 is experiment 21: 300{,}000 random-action games and its policy $\pi_0$ (\texttt{policy-m5}).
Round $k = 1,\dots,4$: play 100{,}000 games in which the learner follows $\pi_{k-1}$, except that with probability
$\varepsilon = 0.2$ per turn it takes a uniformly random action instead (fixed by seed and turn;
\texttt{--driver eps:0.2:<policy>}); then run value iteration on all games so far (round 0 and rounds $1..k$) to get
$\pi_k$. Mixing data from different behaviour policies is fine for the model: each transition is counted under the
action that was actually taken. Each $\pi_k$ plays the 2{,}000 validation seeds against the bot.

\section{Behaviour}
The policy hardly changes over the rounds. On the evaluation seeds round 4 plays bot 27.0\%, expand 15.0\%,
economy 14.3\%, army 17.0\%, research 26.7\% of learner turns (round 0: 28.1, 15.1, 12.2, 15.2, 29.5\%); 92.9\% of its
turns are in a state with a policy entry (round 0: 86.7\%). The new games add states rather than visits: the state
count grows from 506{,}245 to 757{,}621, and the visits per state stay about the same (17 to 25).

\section{Interpretation}
\textbf{Prediction 1 (validation gain rises to at least +250): wrong.} +154, +166, +150, +183, +181: at most a
small rise, within about one standard error. Round 4 is not significantly better than round 0 on the evaluation
seeds either (+14, $p = 0.13$).

\textbf{Prediction 2 (research on more than half the turns): wrong.} The research share stays at 25--27\%.

\textbf{Prediction 3 (optimism falls, below +800 in round 4): direction right, threshold just missed} (+1{,}793
down to +825). The data from the policy's own play makes $V$ more honest about the policy's states.

\textbf{Prediction 4 (passes against probe-perf with +200 to +400; gap to research every turn below 150): half
right.} It passes, but with +118, and only because the 10th percentile is now 5.5 points above the base's instead of
4.5 below (experiment 21): the guard sits on the edge, and the two policies are not significantly different. The gap
to research every turn stays at 338.

\textbf{The explanation tested is not what holds value iteration back, at least not in this form.} The rounds were
meant to bring data into the states a research-heavy player visits. They could not: the policy researches on a
quarter of turns, so its own games (with 20\% uniform exploration) do not go there either; consistent research
streaks stay as rare as in random play. The loop has no reason to drift towards research, so it stays where it
started. What the rounds do show: more data in the policy's own states makes $V$ less optimistic but does not
change the actions, so noise in $Q$ alone does not explain why research is not chosen more. Two readings remain:
(i) value iteration needs data from research-heavy play to see its value (a test: add games played by ``research
every turn'' with exploration to the data), or (ii) on this state the value of researching is not visible turn by
turn, because what research buys (techs that pay off later) shows up in the six integers only weakly.

\section{Threats to validity}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{The PASS is on the edge.} The 10th-percentile guard decides by 5.5 points in this run and by 4.5 the
        other way in experiment 21, for two policies that are not significantly different; a different seed set
        could flip either verdict.
  \item \emph{One $\varepsilon$, four rounds.} $\varepsilon = 0.2$ with uniform exploration; exploration aimed at
        research streaks (or a larger $\varepsilon$) was not tried.
  \item \emph{Gate code not on \texttt{dev} yet} (PR \#198); \texttt{rl-value eval} agrees with it seed for seed.
\end{itemize}

\section{Verdict and next step}
\textbf{Not kept as a method}: rounds of play and refit do not improve the policy significantly over experiment 21
(+14 points, $p = 0.13$). The round-4 policy passes \texttt{rl-gate} against \texttt{greedy-full-probe-perf}
(information under the pass rule of 26 Nov), where 21's policy failed on the 10th-percentile guard by 4.5 points;
the two are equivalent within noise. Either can be David's RL v2 candidate, to be gated against RL v1 (experiment
14); round 4 is the one that passes the baseline gate.

Next step (one idea): test reading (i) directly: add 100{,}000 games played by research every turn with 20\%
random actions to the data and run value iteration once; if the policy then researches much more and gains, the
data's coverage was the limit.

\section{Notes for the paper}
Claim: refitting on the policy's own $\varepsilon$-greedy play makes the values more honest ($V$(start) optimism
+1{,}793 to +825 over four rounds) but leaves the policy and its score unchanged (+14 points, not significant): the
loop cannot explore towards a behaviour (research streaks) its own policy rarely shows. An example of why
exploration must be aimed, not just added.
