\section{Question and hypothesis}
\textbf{The idea (David, 26 Nov 2026, issue \#166).} Experiment 17 tuned the gate's greedy bots for Perfection, and
\texttt{greedy-full-probe-perf} is now the learner's base bot for both tracks and the frozen anchor. Re-run experiment
16 on top of it, so the value function describes the bot the RL agent will steer. Method unchanged: the state is six
exact integers (turn, cities, income, units, sum of city levels, techs), no bins; $V(s_t) = \mathbb{E}[\text{final
score} - \text{score}_t \mid s_t]$, the remaining score of the bot's own play.

\textbf{Prediction} (before the run; from experiment 16, adjusted for a bot tuned for this mode):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Coverage stays high:} 95--98\% of held-out learner turns land in a state seen in the 300{,}000
        training games (16: 97.0\%).
  \item \emph{Same overall picture:} model-based $V$ is 9--14\% below the turn baseline in RMSE over all held-out
        turns (16: 11.9\%), and 22--30\% below it at turns 20--25 (16: 27.6\% and 25.9\%).
  \item \emph{Same split between the estimators:} Monte Carlo is better in the early turns, the model-based $V$ in
        the late turns, with the crossover between turns 12 and 19 (16: turn 16).
  \item \emph{The bot does slightly better:} mean held-out final score 6{,}800--7{,}000 (16: 6{,}747; the gate's
        baseline on the evaluation seeds: 6{,}839), eliminations 24--30\% (16: 27.6\%).
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Against experiments 13 and 16:} if the model-based $V$ is not below the turn baseline or the estimator split
reverses, their result was specific to the untuned bot.

\section{Mechanism}
As experiment 13 (\texttt{rl-value collect} and \texttt{fit}, \texttt{engine/rlvalue}): the state is read at the start
of each learner turn; reward = change in raw score; three estimators on the same training games: model-based
(certainty equivalence: transitions $(r, s')$ per state, backward induction from the last turn), Monte Carlo
(mean return-to-go per state) and the turn baseline (mean return-to-go per turn). A held-out state not seen in
training gets the turn baseline. New in the tool: the terminal row records whether the learner won, so the report
gives the win rate.

\section{Behaviour}
Not a policy change. The tuned bot's games look like the untuned bot's: a quarter of the learners are eliminated
before turn 30 and the learner wins about 3\% of games against three Crazy opponents (now Perfection-tuned
themselves). The learner scores slightly more (median 8{,}130 against 7{,}925 in experiment 16). Visits concentrate as
before: 96.8\% of held-out turns land in a state seen in training, 87.8\% in one seen at least 10 times.

\section{Interpretation}
\textbf{Prediction 1 (coverage 95--98\%): right} (96.8\%).

\textbf{Prediction 2 (overall gain 9--14\%, 22--30\% at turns 20--25): right} (12.2\%; 27.2\% at turn 20, 25.8\% at
turn 25).

\textbf{Prediction 3 (Monte Carlo early, model late, crossover between turns 12 and 19): right} (crossover at turn
14, two turns earlier than in 16).

\textbf{Prediction 4 (mean 6{,}800--7{,}000, eliminations 24--30\%): right} (6{,}895; 27.2\%).

The result of experiments 13 and 16 is not specific to the untuned bot: with the Perfection-tuned learner and
opponents, the six integers predict the remaining score about as well (a quarter better than the turn alone from
turn 14 to 25, about 47\% of the variance), and the estimator split is the same. The state is not Markov (map,
opponents and positions are hidden), so backward induction through the learned model is biased early, where Monte
Carlo has enough visits to win; from turn 14 the model's pooling over successors wins.

\section{Threats to validity}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{One policy}: the values are those of \texttt{greedy-full-probe-perf} on this setup; a learner that
        plays differently visits other states (the value-iteration policy of experiment 21 is such a learner).
  \item \emph{Overall RMSE} weights the uninformative early turns as much as the late ones; the per-turn table is the
        fairer measure.
  \item \emph{The comparison with 16} changes learner and opponents at once, so the small differences in score and
        crossover cannot be attributed to either.
\end{itemize}

\section{Verdict and next step}
A measurement, no gate (no new player): the full-state value function works on the new base bot as on the old one.
Done. It is the reference for experiment 21 (value iteration on top of probe-perf) and for Jakob's cities-only
value function (experiment 14) on the same held-out games.
